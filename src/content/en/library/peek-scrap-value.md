## 1. Is it really PEEK?

The PAEK family looks alike as scrap. PEEK (VICTREX 450G) melts at 343 °C; PEK (VICTREX HT G22) at 373 °C; PEKEKK (VICTREX ST G45) at 387 °C; Kepstan PEKK at 325–336 °C (7000 series) or 355–360 °C (8000 series), with the 6000 series lower still; and LMPAEK at 305 °C ([materials data reference](/materials/#paek)). A DSC run confirms the polymer, but close melting points need more than DSC — which is why a known grade name is worth so much.

## 2. Grade and filler

Unfilled, carbon-fiber, glass-fiber and lubricated (PTFE, graphite) grades are different products with different buyers. A lot that mixes them is worth less than the same material sorted. TGA measures filler content; a mixed lot shows up immediately.

## 3. Cleanliness

Clean, dry swarf and offcuts are worth the most. Coolant- or oil-wet chips can often be cleaned, but cleaning costs money and the price reflects it. Metal fragments, paper, floor sweepings and other plastics reduce value further and can make a lot unusable for melt reprocessing.

## 4. Segregation and labeling

The single cheapest thing a shop can do to raise the value of its PEEK scrap is to keep each grade in its own container, labeled with the grade name — for example "450G swarf, unfilled" or "450CA30 rod ends".

## 5. Form

Large, clean pieces — rod and plate ends, tube offcuts — are easy to identify and process. Fine swarf is valuable but carries more surface contamination. Molding runners and sprues are usually clean and single-grade. Purge varies widely and should be kept separate from everything else.

## 6. Molecular weight and crystallinity

PEEK that has seen long or repeated heat history may have lost molecular weight; melt flow testing shows this. Crystallinity also varies — quenched material can be low in crystallinity without being degraded, and annealing can recover it. DSC tells the two apart, which matters when a lot looks "off" in color or clarity.

## 7. Volume and regularity

A recurring, predictable stream is worth more than a one-off lot of the same size, because it can be planned into production. Our typical minimum for PAEK lots is 50–100 kg, and small regular streams can be collected over time.
