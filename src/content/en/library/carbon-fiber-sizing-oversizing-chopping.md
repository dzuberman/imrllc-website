## What sizing is

Carbon fiber leaves the producer with a **sizing** — a thin polymer coating, typically around one percent of the fiber weight or less, applied to protect the filaments during handling and to promote bonding to the matrix. TORAYCA T700S, a widely used PAN-based fiber, is sold with three standard sizings: 50C at 1.0% (listed for epoxy, phenolic, polyester and vinyl ester), 60E at 0.2% (epoxy) and F0E at 0.6% (vinyl ester and epoxy) ([T700S datasheet](https://www.toraycma.com/wp-content/uploads/T700S-Data-Sheet.pdf)).

As that list shows, standard sizings are developed for **thermoset** resins.

## Why it matters for thermoplastics

Short-fiber thermoplastic compounds are made at the melt temperature of the polymer — for high-performance polymers such as PEEK, PEI or PPS, that is far above where most thermoset resins are processed. Two things follow:

- **Interface.** How well fiber and polymer bond depends on whether the sizing chemistry is compatible with the matrix. Fiber–matrix adhesion is what turns fiber loading into stiffness and strength.
- **Volatiles.** Sizing chemistry that is not stable at the processing temperature can release volatiles in the extruder, which must be vented.

Fiber producers offer sizings intended for thermoplastic matrices, and compounders choose fiber with the matrix and processing temperature in mind. When recovered fiber is the feedstock, its sizing history is part of the material's specification — which is why we ask sellers for the fiber grade and sizing, and buyers for their target matrix and process.

## What oversizing is

**Oversizing** is a secondary binder coating applied on top of a fiber's existing sizing. Its job on a chopping line is mechanical: it holds the filaments of each bundle together so the bundle cuts cleanly to a precise length and stays intact as it is fed into a compounder, instead of fluffing into loose filaments that bridge and feed unevenly.

## How different carbon fiber waste is chopped

| Waste form | Treatment | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Loose roving, off-grade bobbins | Oversizing, then precision chopping | Bundles cut to a set length (our lines: 0.1–12 mm) |
| Other dry fiber — selvedge, fabric offcuts, short or mixed tow | Random chopping | Fiber in a length range |
| Prepreg trim, cured composite scrap | Random chopping in some cases; not resized | Resin stays on the fiber, limiting where it can be used |

## For buyers

Tell your supplier the matrix polymer, the processing temperature, the compounding equipment and the target fiber length and loading. For recovered fiber, ask for the source fiber type, its original sizing where known, the oversize applied and the cut-length distribution.
