## Two products, one source

Both regrind and repelletized granule start as the same scrap — runners, sprues, purge, rejected parts, machining swarf or offcuts. What separates them is whether the material has been melted again.

**Regrind** is the product of mechanical size reduction alone. Scrap is fed through a granulator or shredder and comes out as irregular flakes and chips, sized to feed an extruder or molding machine. Its particle shape and size vary, and it carries whatever the scrap carried: moisture, fines, dust, and any variation between parts or batches.

**Repelletized granule** — also called regranulate or reprocessed pellet — has been through the melt. Regrind is dried, melted and homogenized in an extruder, pushed through a die, cooled (usually in a water bath) and cut into uniform cylindrical or lens-shaped pellets.

## Why the difference matters

| | Regrind | Repelletized granule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Shape and size | Irregular flakes, fines | Uniform pellets |
| Feeding | Can bridge in hoppers; uneven dosing | Feeds like virgin resin |
| Bulk density | Lower and variable | Close to virgin pellets |
| Homogeneity | Batch-to-batch and part-to-part variation remains | Melt-mixed into one consistent lot |
| Heat history | As molded or machined | One more melt cycle |
| Typical use | In-house reuse, blending at a controlled ratio | Production molding and extrusion, compounding |

A molder blending a few percent of its own regrind back into virgin material usually doesn't need pellets. A buyer who wants to run recycled material as its main feedstock usually does: uniform pellets feed, dry and melt predictably.

## The extra melt cycle

Repelletizing adds one more heat history, and each melt cycle can change molecular weight. For high-performance polymers this is why recyclers track **melt flow** from cycle to cycle: a rising melt flow indicates chain scission. Good practice is to dry the regrind properly, keep residence time at temperature short, and blend to a target specification. DSC after processing confirms that crystallinity and melting behavior are where they should be.
