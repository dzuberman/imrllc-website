## Route 1 — Melt route

**For:** polymers that melt — PEEK, PEKK, PEK, LMPAEK, PEI, TPI, PSU, PESU, PPSU, PPS, LCP, PFA, FEP, ETFE, ECTFE, PVDF, PCTFE, HT polyamides and uncured PAI.

Scrap is mechanically size-reduced to regrind, cleaned where needed, dried, and tested by DSC, TGA and melt flow. A twin-screw extruder re-homogenizes and degasses the melt; the strand is water-bath cooled and cut into uniform granule. Melt flow is tracked from cycle to cycle, and regrind is blended to hold a target specification.

**Outputs:** regrind for customers who process it themselves; neat repelletized granule; compound granule with carbon fiber, glass fiber, mineral or functional additives compounded in-line.

## Route 2 — Powder route

**For:** materials that cannot be melt-reprocessed — thermoset polyimide (Vespel®, Kapton®, P84®), fully post-cured PAI, PTFE, PBI, phenolic and elastomers (FKM, FFKM).

The grinding process is chosen by the material's mechanical state at room temperature:

- **Ambient mechanical milling** for glassy, brittle materials — thermoset polyimide, post-cured PAI, PBI, phenolic. They fracture cleanly without cooling.
- **Jet milling after molecular-weight reduction** for PTFE, producing micropowder.
- **Cryogenic grinding** only where it is needed: elastomers, which smear at room temperature, and drawn, highly oriented fibers such as UHMWPE and para-aramid.

**Outputs:** fine powder used as a functional filler — adding wear resistance, lubricity or temperature resistance to compounds — and coarser powder for compression molding.

## Route 3 — Fiber route

**For:** carbon fiber, para-aramid, UHMWPE, PBO, PBI, oxidized PAN, phenolic and high-performance polymer fibers.

After cleaning and opening, fiber is precision-cut, pulped, spun or made into nonwovens. Loose carbon fiber roving and off-grade bobbins are **oversized** — given a secondary binder that holds each bundle together — so they chop cleanly to length between 0.1 and 12 mm; other dry carbon fiber is random-chopped. See [carbon fiber](/carbon-fiber/) and [aramid fibers](/aramid-fiber-recycling/).

## Testing and documentation

| Step | What we measure | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Intake | DSC (glass transition, melting point, crystallinity), TGA (filler and ash content), melt flow, density | Confirms identity and condition before processing |
| Process | Melt flow and rheology per cycle | Tracks molecular-weight change; sets blend ratios |
| Release | Certificate of Analysis for every shipment, traceable to source stream and run | Lets you qualify and trace each lot |

## Cross-border handling

Scrap and recovered materials move between our sites and customers under the waste and customs rules of each country involved. We classify each lot, prepare notifications and customs documents where required, and keep chain-of-custody records, and we provide documentation to support customers' aerospace, defense and sustainability reporting requirements.
