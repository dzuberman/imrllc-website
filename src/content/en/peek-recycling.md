## What we recycle

PEEK (polyether ether ketone) is the highest-value stream most machine shops and molders generate, and it recycles well when it is kept clean and segregated. We buy:

- **Machining swarf and chips** from rod, plate and tube — the largest PEEK scrap stream in most shops.
- **Stock-shape ends and offcuts** — rod ends, plate trim, tube cut-offs.
- **Molding scrap** — runners, sprues, purge and rejected parts.
- **Regrind, off-spec pellets and powder.**

We take the whole PAEK family: PEEK, PEKK, PEK, PEKEKK, LMPAEK and PAEK copolymers such as AvaSpire®. Unfilled, carbon-fiber, glass-fiber and lubricated (PTFE, graphite) grades are all accepted, as long as each grade is kept in its own container.

## How PEEK scrap is recovered

PEEK melts at about 343 °C (VICTREX 450G datasheet), so it follows the **melt route**:

1. **Intake testing.** DSC confirms the polymer by its melting point and shows crystallinity; TGA measures filler content; melt flow shows how much molecular weight the lot has kept.
2. **Cleaning where needed.** Coolant- or oil-wet swarf is cleaned before reprocessing.
3. **Size reduction and drying** to a uniform regrind.
4. **Twin-screw extrusion** to repelletized granule — neat, or compounded with carbon fiber, glass fiber or tribological additives.
5. **Release testing** and a Certificate of Analysis with every shipment.

Each reprocessing cycle can shift melt flow, so we track it from lot to lot and blend to a target specification.

## Telling the PAEKs apart

The PAEK polymers look alike as scrap but melt at different temperatures. Typical datasheet values:

| Polymer | Reference grade | Tm (°C) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| PEEK | VICTREX 450G | 343 |
| PEK | VICTREX HT G22 | 373 |
| PEKEKK | VICTREX ST G45 | 387 |
| PEKK 7000 series | Kepstan 7002 | 336 |
| PEKK 8000 series | Kepstan 8002 | 360 |
| LMPAEK | VICTREX LMPAEK 101 | 305 |

Sources are listed in the [materials data reference](/materials/#paek). Close melting points — PEEK and AvaSpire AV-651 (345 °C), for example — need more than DSC to separate, which is why the original grade name on your label is so useful.

## What determines the price

Grade and filler, cleanliness, segregation, form, quantity and how regularly it is available. Clean, single-grade swarf with a known grade name is worth the most; mixed, oily or metal-contaminated material is worth less or needs cleaning first. Our guide [What determines the value of PEEK scrap](/library/peek-scrap-value/) explains each factor.
