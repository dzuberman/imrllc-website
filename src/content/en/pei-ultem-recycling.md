## What we recycle

PEI is an amorphous, melt-processable imidized polymer. ULTEM 1000 has a glass transition of 217 °C, a density of 1.27 g/cm³ and an oxygen index of 47% (SABIC datasheet), and PEI is widely used in aircraft interiors, electrical components, medical devices and semiconductor fixtures. We buy:

- Machining swarf and offcuts from rod, plate and tube.
- Injection-molding runners, sprues, purge and rejected parts.
- Sheet and film trim.
- Additive-manufacturing waste — filament, supports, failed builds and powder.

Unfilled and glass- or carbon-filled grades are accepted; keep each grade separate.

## How PEI scrap is recovered

PEI follows the **melt route**: intake testing (DSC for glass transition, TGA for filler content, melt flow), cleaning where needed, drying, size reduction and twin-screw extrusion to repelletized granule or compound. Because PEI is amorphous, DSC shows a glass transition and no melting peak — one of the checks that separates it from look-alike polymers.

## Keep PEI separate from

- **PSU, PESU and PPSU** — also amber and amorphous; PEI's 217 °C glass transition sits between PSU (185 °C) and PESU/PPSU (220 °C), so mixed lots are hard to sort after the fact.
- **TPI and thermoset polyimide** — related chemistry, different recovery routes. See [polyimide and PBI recycling](/polyimide-pbi-recycling/).
