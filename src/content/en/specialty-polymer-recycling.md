## Liquid crystal polymer (LCP)

LCP is used for thin-wall, precise electrical and electronic parts. VECTRA® A950 has a density of 1.40 g/cm³ and a heat deflection temperature of 180 °C at 1.8 MPa ([materials data reference](/materials/#others)). LCP's very low melt viscosity and strongly oriented flow make it demanding to reprocess, so lots are assessed individually. Production lumps and purge from LCP producers and compounders are a regular part of our [by-product collection](/producer-by-products/).

## Polyphthalamide (PPA) and semi-aromatic polyamides

PPA and related semi-aromatic polyamides (PA6T, PA9T, PA10T copolymers) bridge standard nylons and the high-temperature families; they are common in automotive under-hood and electrical parts. A 33% glass-filled Amodel® grade, AE-4133, lists a melting point of 327 °C. PPA lumps and off-spec material from production are welcome.

## Aliphatic polyketone (PK)

PK is an alternating copolymer of carbon monoxide with ethylene and propylene, chemically unrelated to the aromatic PAEKs despite the name. Hyosung's POKETONE® M330F lists a melting point of 222 °C and a density of 1.24 g/cm³. It is valued for wear and fuel resistance.

## Specialty polyamides

PA46, long-chain polyamides (PA11, PA12, PA610, PA612, PA1010, PA1012), PA410, MXD6, transparent amorphous polyamides and polyether block amides (PEBA). These need careful segregation — several long-chain polyamides are hard to tell apart without spectroscopy — and are recycled by the melt route or traded to processors that specialize in them.

## Engineering polyesters

Semi-crystalline PET (machining grades such as PET-P), PBT, PCT and PEN, and amorphous copolyesters such as PETG and PCTG. Polyesters must be dried thoroughly before any melt processing, because moisture breaks the chains at processing temperature.

Send photos, the grade name and the quantity, and we will tell you which route fits.
