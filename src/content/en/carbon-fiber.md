## Three kinds of carbon fiber waste, three treatments

How carbon fiber waste can be reused depends on what state it is in.

**Loose roving and off-grade bobbins → oversizing and precision chopping.** This is continuous, still-sized fiber: bobbin ends, off-spec spools, loose and tangled tow. Before chopping, we apply an **oversize** — a secondary binder coating that holds the filaments of each bundle together. Bundles that stay intact cut cleanly to a specified length and feed evenly into a compounding line. Our precision-chop lines cut from 0.1 to 12 mm.

**Other dry carbon fiber → random chopping.** Selvedge, dry fabric offcuts and short or mixed tow are random-chopped to a length range rather than one exact length.

**Prepreg trim and cured composite scrap → limited options.** This fiber is still bound in resin. In some cases it can be random-chopped, but it is not resized: the resin stays on the fiber, and that restricts where it can be used. We assess these streams case by case.

## Why sizing matters

Carbon fiber leaves the producer with a sizing — a thin coating that protects the filaments and bonds them to the matrix. Standard sizings are designed for thermoset resins: Toray, for example, lists the standard sizings of TORAYCA T700S for epoxy, phenolic, polyester and vinyl ester matrices ([T700S datasheet](https://www.toraycma.com/wp-content/uploads/T700S-Data-Sheet.pdf)). When fiber goes into a thermoplastic processed at high temperature — PEEK, PEI or PPS — the sizing chemistry influences how well fiber and polymer bond and how much volatile matter is released during compounding.

That is why we ask buyers for their **target matrix and process**: it tells us whether a given oversize and cut length suit the job. Our guide [Carbon fiber sizing, oversizing and chopping](/library/carbon-fiber-sizing-oversizing-chopping/) goes deeper.

## Selling carbon fiber waste

Tell us the fiber grade (for example T700S), the sizing type, the tow size, the form (bobbins, loose tow, selvedge, fabric, prepreg) and the quantity, with photos. Producer-labeled bobbins with intact labels are the easiest to place.
