## Two kinds of PAI scrap

PAI is molded as a thermoplastic and then **post-cured**: parts go through an extended, staged heat cycle that develops their full strength, wear and chemical resistance. The Torlon PAI design guide describes cure studies at 260 °C and notes that the length of the cycle depends on part configuration and thickness. That cure divides PAI scrap into two streams:

- **Uncured scrap** — runners, sprues and off-spec parts collected at the molding press, before post-cure. It can still be melt-reprocessed.
- **Post-cured scrap** — machining swarf, rod and plate ends and finished parts. It no longer melt-processes and is recovered by **micronization** into fine powder, used as a functional filler. The group micronizes cured PAI in the US, Croatia and India.

Mixing the two lowers the value of both. Our guide [Cured and uncured PAI scrap](/library/pai-torlon-scrap-cured-vs-uncured/) explains how to keep them apart.

| --- | --- |
| Torlon 4203L | Unfilled |
| Torlon 4301 | 12% graphite, 3% PTFE (bearing grade) |

Sources: Drake Plastics Torlon datasheets and the Torlon PAI design guide — see the [materials data reference](/materials/#imidized). Keep each grade in its own container; filled and lubricated grades are worth sorting.

## What to send us

Photos, the grade name, whether the material is cured or uncured, the form (swarf, rod ends, runners, parts) and the quantity. If you can't tell whether a lot is cured, tell us where in your process it was collected — at the press or after machining.
