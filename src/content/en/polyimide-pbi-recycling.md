## Thermoset polyimide (Vespel®)

Thermoset polyimide does not melt. Parts and stock shapes are made by compression and sintering, and DuPont lists long-term performance for Vespel® SP up to 260 °C. Machining generates valuable swarf and chips, which we recover by **ambient mechanical milling**: the material is glassy and brittle at room temperature, so it fractures cleanly without cryogenic cooling. The powder is used as a functional filler that adds temperature and wear resistance to compounds.

Grades matter, because fillers travel with the powder:

| Vespel® grade | Composition |
| --- | --- |
| SP-1 | Unfilled |
| SP-21 | 15% graphite |
| SP-22 | 40% graphite |
| SP-211 | 15% graphite + 10% PTFE |

Source: DuPont Vespel SP datasheet — see the [materials data reference](/materials/#imidized). Polyimide swarf can carry fine metal from the machining process; tell us how it was machined so we can plan cleaning and screening.

## Thermoplastic polyimide (TPI)

TPI such as AURUM® is melt-processable. Mitsui lists a glass transition of 250 °C, and its JCN3030 grade melts at 388 °C, so TPI scrap goes through the **melt route** like PEEK, at higher temperatures. Keep it separate from the high-melting PAEKs: PEKEKK (VICTREX ST G45) melts at 387 °C, almost the same temperature, so DSC alone cannot tell them apart.

## PBI (Celazole®)

PBI is a polybenzimidazole — not a polyimide — with a glass transition of 427 °C and a limiting oxygen index of 58% (Celazole U-60SD datasheet). It is compression-molded, not injection-molded, and its scrap is recovered by ambient milling to fine powder for high-temperature compounds.

## Film and fiber

Polyimide film (Kapton®) and polyimide fiber (P84®) are handled case by case; send photos and the source process.
